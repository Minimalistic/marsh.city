Slide 1 of 9: WhatCanHelp

On the slide: Finding assistive technology, what it costs, and how to pay for it. Jason Marsh, a personal project. https://whatcanhelp.com. Scan to follow along.

What Jason said: Thanks for having me. I'm Jason Marsh, Director of Technology at the Lighthouse in Duluth. WhatCanHelp is a personal project, built on my own time. It came from seeing how hard it is for families, and the professionals helping them, to work out what exists, what it costs, and how to pay for it.

Slide 2 of 9: A free, current catalog of assistive technology.

On the slide: whatcanhelp.com

What Jason said: It's a free catalog of assistive tech, pulled from vendors and kept current automatically. There are no ads, no affiliate links, and no accounts.

Slide 3 of 9: Not sure where to start? Match asks a few questions.

On the slide: whatcanhelp.com/intake

What Jason said: If you don't know where to start, Match asks a few questions about the situation. Here, someone's dad is 82, has trouble following conversations at dinner and hearing the TV, and isn't ready for hearing aids. Each result has a plain-language description, a complexity rating, and the price.

Slide 4 of 9: Borrow before you buy, in every state.

On the slide: whatcanhelp.com/borrow

What Jason said: Before anyone buys anything, product pages point to borrowing first, through the state's AT Act program. Every state and territory is listed. Here it's set to Minnesota, so it shows STAR and PACER, with the loan inventory and phone number. Each state also has a page of where to start with funding.

Slide 5 of 9: More than 4,500 products, rechecked against vendor sites.

On the slide: whatcanhelp.com/browse

What Jason said: Everything is browsable by need. This is mobility and movement, about 520 products: everyday things like utensils, shoe horns and canes, not just high-tech devices. Every card shows the maker, the price, how much setup it takes, and when it was last checked against the vendor's site.

Slide 6 of 9: Every product gets a plain-language summary.

What Jason said: Vendor pages are written to sell. Each summary is generated from the vendor's own published content and rewritten in plain language: what it is, who it helps, and how it's used. This is the BIGmack from AbleNet. The summary is labeled AI-generated and dated, and terms like switch link to the glossary.

Slide 7 of 9: 135 terms, explained where they appear.

What Jason said: Jargon is its own barrier. The glossary has 135 terms in 13 groups, from AAC to funding and policy. On product pages, terms like switch are underlined, and tapping one explains it right there, without leaving the page.

Slide 8 of 9: Tagged by need, type, platform, age, complexity, price and funding.

What Jason said: Each product is classified across the same dimensions professionals think in, so a search for a communication need finds communication products. The complexity rating says up front how much setup it takes. Funding includes AT Act lending, meaning the state's loan program. Every page says where its facts came from, when they were last checked, and how confident the classification is, with a reminder to verify before clinical or funding use. Corrections from people like you are how it gets better.
