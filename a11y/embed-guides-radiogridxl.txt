17. RadioGrid is built for the person listening: big buttons, one per station, nothing else to figure out. Everything you can change is tucked away in a hidden settings panel, so the listener never lands there by accident.
18. RadioGrid saves your stations and settings inside the web browser you’re using, on this device only.
19. Nothing is sent anywhere, and there’s no account. If you open RadioGrid on another phone or computer, or in a different browser, it starts fresh with its two starting stations. The same happens if you clear your browser’s saved data for marsh.city, the website RadioGrid runs on. To move a setup between devices, use
20. link: Backup and restore
21. . On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app counts as a different browser, so

23. before you set it up.

231. heading level 3: Use it by voice
232. Someone who can’t touch the screen can run the radio by speaking, with the Voice Control built into iPhone, iPad and Mac. Whoever sets the radio up turns it on: on an iPhone or iPad,
233. Settings › Accessibility › Voice Control
234. ; on a Mac,

238. Voice Control › Overlay › Item Numbers
239. : every button then keeps a small number beside it, and “Tap 2” presses button 2, whatever it’s called. On a Mac, say “Show numbers” first, then the number.
240. Numbers and names on screen need sight. For a listener who can’t see the screen, use VoiceOver (
241. Settings › Accessibility › VoiceOver
242. ), which reads each station and button aloud.
243. Buttons can also be pressed by what they show:
244. [list, 4 items] “Tap” and a station’s full name, such as “Tap Jazz 88”, plays it. While a station is paused, use its number to resume it.
245. “Tap Stop” stops the station. With Advanced controls, “Tap Pause”, “Tap Sleep”, and for recordings “Tap Back 15” and “Tap Skip 30”.
246. “Tap Settings” opens settings, unless the Settings button has been hidden.
247. “Show names” labels every button with what to say.
248. For saying stations by name, give each one a short name that’s easy to say and different from the others. Settings warns when two stations share a name, or when a station is named like a button, such as Stop.
