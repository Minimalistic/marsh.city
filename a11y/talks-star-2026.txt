51. link: whatcanhelp.com/browse

53. On a tablet: the catalog filtered to Vision and sight, showing 24 of 3,302 products as a grid of tiles. The first rows are the Daylight Smart Clip-on Lamp ($37.50, Ready to use), the Google Pixel 11 Pro (estimated $999 to $1,299, Setup with instructions), the Beecher Mirage 6x30 Binocular for Distance Viewing ($695.00, Setup with instructions) and the Complete Braille Sudoku Set ($49.95, Ready to use).
54. link: Full-size image: catalog (PNG)
55. What Jason said: Everything is browsable by need. This is Vision & sight, about 3,300 of them on its own. Every card shows the maker, the current price and how much setup it takes.
56. heading level 2: Slide 6 of 9: Every product gets a plain-language summary.
57. image: Screenshot: product page
58. On a tablet: the product page for the Pocketalker 2.0 by Williams Sound, with its photo, the price of $250.00 and a Ready to use complexity rating.
59. link: Full-size image: product page (PNG)
60. image: Screenshot: product summary
61. On a phone: the Pocketalker's plain-language summary, labeled AI-generated from vendor-published content and dated, with glossary links for personal amplifier, hearing aids and telecoil.
62. link: Full-size image: product summary (PNG)
63. What Jason said: Vendor pages are written to sell. Each summary is generated from the vendor's own published content and rewritten in plain language: what it is, who it helps, and how it's used. It's labeled AI-generated and dated, and technical terms like telecoil link to the glossary.
64. heading level 2: Slide 7 of 9: 135 terms, explained where they appear.
65. image: Screenshot: glossary
66. On a tablet: the Glossary of Assistive Technology, with plain-language definitions for 135 terms, a filter box, buttons for each topic group, and the first Communication and AAC terms with short definitions.
67. link: Full-size image: glossary (PNG)
68. image: Screenshot: glossary popover
69. On a phone: the Pocketalker product page with the glossary popover for telecoil open, explaining it as a small wire coil inside a hearing aid or cochlear implant that picks up audio from hearing loops, with a link to the full glossary.
70. link: Full-size image: glossary popover (PNG)
71. What Jason said: Jargon is its own barrier. The glossary has 135 terms in 13 groups, from AAC to funding and policy. On product pages, terms like telecoil are underlined, and tapping one explains it right there, without leaving the page.
72. heading level 2: Slide 8 of 9: Tagged by need, type, platform, age, complexity, price and funding.
73. image: Screenshot: quick facts
74. On a tablet: the Pocketalker's quick facts. Type: assistive listening systems. Addresses: hearing. Platform: dedicated device. Age range: all ages. Complexity: ready to use. Price: $250.00. Funding: AT Act lending, out of pocket, vocational rehab.
75. link: Full-size image: quick facts (PNG)
76. image: Screenshot: sources and fine print
77. Close-up of the Sources and fine print box: vendor facts sourced from LS&S, last checked October 21, 2026, and classification AI-generated from vendor content with high confidence, with a reminder to verify before clinical or funding use.
78. link: Full-size image: sources and fine print (PNG)
79. What Jason said: Each product is classified across the same dimensions professionals think in, so a search for a hearing need finds hearing products. The complexity rating says up front whether it's ready to use or needs setup with instructions. Every page says where its facts came from, when they were last checked, and how confident the classification is, with a reminder to verify before clinical or funding use. Corrections from people like you are how it gets better.
