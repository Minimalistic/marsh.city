30. RadioGrid is built for the person listening: big buttons, one per station, nothing else to figure out. Everything you can change is tucked away in a hidden settings panel, so the listener never lands there by accident.
31. RadioGrid saves your stations and settings inside the web browser you’re using, on this device only.
32. Nothing is sent anywhere, and there’s no account. If you open RadioGrid on another phone or computer, or in a different browser, it starts fresh with its two starting stations. The same happens if you clear your browser’s saved data for marsh.city, the website RadioGrid runs on. To move a setup between devices, use
33. link: Backup and restore
34. . On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app counts as a different browser, so
35. link: add it to your Home Screen
36. before you set it up.
37. heading level 2: Listening
38. image: The main screen with two large station buttons
39. Tap a station to play it, and tap it again to pause. The dot in the top right corner turns green while a station is playing. At the bottom of the screen there’s a Stop button.
40. heading level 2: Open settings
41. To open settings, tap
42. Settings
43. in the top corner. The button is outlined on the screenshot above.
44. If there is no Settings button, it has been hidden: tap the logo in the top corner 5 times quickly instead. See

48. The panel slides in from the side. It has four groups:
49. Stations
50. ,

55. Setup
56. . Scroll to see them all. Most changes show up behind the panel right away, so you can see what they’ll look like, and when one tap changes other settings too (a preset, say), those settings light up briefly so you can see what moved.
57. Nothing is saved until you tap Apply & Close
58. , which stays pinned at the bottom of the panel (on a phone turned sideways, at the top next to Preview). If you close the panel with the × instead, the app asks whether to throw your changes away.
59. image: The Apply & Close button pinned at the bottom of settings
60. If settings sit untouched for 3 minutes, the app asks
61. Still setting up?
62. Tap anywhere on it to carry on. If nobody taps within 30 seconds, settings close and go back to the stations, and changes you haven’t applied are thrown away, so the listener is never left looking at settings. A station that was playing keeps playing, and a paused one stays paused. The PIN keypad closes by itself after a minute with no tap.
63. heading level 3: If it asks for a PIN
64. image: The PIN keypad
65. If someone has set a PIN, opening settings brings up a keypad instead. Enter the 4-digit PIN to get in. After 5 wrong tries the keypad locks for a little while and counts down the seconds, and the wait gets longer each time. See
66. link: Lock settings with a PIN
67. to set or remove one.

It’s off unless you turn it on, so a hand that pinches by accident can’t leave the radio zoomed in. The zoom built into the phone or tablet itself (Zoom on iPhone and iPad, Magnification on Android) works either way.

244. heading level 3: Use it by voice
245. Someone who can’t touch the screen can run the radio by speaking, with the Voice Control built into iPhone, iPad and Mac. Whoever sets the radio up turns it on: on an iPhone or iPad,
246. Settings › Accessibility › Voice Control
247. ; on a Mac,
248. System Settings › Accessibility › Voice Control
249. . It needs the device’s microphone, and it listens on the device.
250. The surest way is by number. On an iPhone or iPad, also turn on
251. Voice Control › Overlay › Item Numbers
252. : every button then keeps a small number beside it, and “Tap 2” presses button 2, whatever it’s called. On a Mac, say “Show numbers” first, then the number.
253. Numbers and names on screen need sight. For a listener who can’t see the screen, use VoiceOver (
254. Settings › Accessibility › VoiceOver
255. ), which reads each station and button aloud.
256. Buttons can also be pressed by what they show:
257. [list, 4 items] “Tap” and a station’s full name, such as “Tap Jazz 88”, plays it. While a station is paused, use its number to resume it.
258. “Tap Stop” stops the station. With Advanced controls, “Tap Pause”, “Tap Sleep”, and for recordings “Tap Back 15” and “Tap Skip 30”.
259. “Tap Settings” opens settings, unless the Settings button has been hidden.
260. “Show names” labels every button with what to say.
261. For saying stations by name, give each one a short name that’s easy to say and different from the others. Settings warns when two stations share a name, or when a station is named like a button, such as Stop.

279. heading level 2: Lock settings with a PIN

288. Write the PIN down somewhere. If you forget it, the only way back into settings is to clear your browser’s saved data for marsh.city, which also erases your RadioGrid stations and settings.
289. heading level 2: Hide the Settings button
290. image: The Show a Settings button switch in settings, turned off
291. Once the radio is set up, you can hide the Settings button, so the listener can’t open settings by mistake and change things. Turn off
292. Show a Settings button
293. at the top of
294. Setup
295. .
296. image: The practice step: a logo to tap five times before the button is hidden
297. The radio first asks you to practise the way back in.
298. Tap the logo it shows 5 times quickly.
299. When it says
300. Got it
301. , tap
302. Done
303. , then
304. Apply & Close
305. . If the taps are hard to do, tap
306. Cancel
307. and keep the button.
308. With the button hidden, open settings by
309. tapping the logo in the top corner 5 times quickly
310. , within about two seconds. To bring the button back, open settings that way and turn the switch on again.
311. Five quick taps are hard with a switch, by voice or with a screen reader. For someone who sets up their own radio that way, leave the button showing and use a PIN instead.
312. A radio that was set up before the Settings button existed keeps its settings behind the logo until someone turns the switch on.
313. heading level 2: Backup and restore
314. image: The Backup section
315. [list, 2 items] Export Backup
316. saves your stations and settings to a file.
317. Import Backup
318. loads a backup file, on this device or another one.
319. The PIN is only included if you tick
320. Include the PIN
321. . Anyone with that file could get into settings, so keep it somewhere safe.
322. heading level 2: Install it as an app
323. RadioGrid can sit on your home screen like a regular app and open full screen:
324. [list, 3 items] iPhone or iPad (Safari):
325. tap the Share button, then

337. click the install icon at the right end of the address bar.
338. On Android and on computers, the installed app keeps the same stations and settings as the browser you installed it from. On iPhone and iPad it starts fresh, so set up your stations in the Home Screen app. If you already set them up in Safari,
339. link: Backup and restore
340. moves them across.
341. heading level 2: Send us feedback
