11. heading level 1: WhatCanHelp
12. shipped Updated Oct 2026 Written by
13. link: Jason Marsh, resume
14. A free tool for finding assistive technology, borrowing it before you buy, and working out who might pay for it.
15. link: Visit the live site
16. I’m the Director of Technology at Lighthouse Center for Vital Living, a nonprofit in Duluth that serves people with disabilities and older adults. Part of that job is assistive technology: evaluating devices, setting them up for clients, running an AT lending library, and answering colleagues’ questions about what works. WhatCanHelp started from a question I kept coming back to: what would it look like if the AT industry had better discovery tools?
17. It’s a free site with thousands of assistive technology products from hundreds of manufacturers in one place. You can browse the catalog, or describe a situation and get a shortlist. It also points you to the free programs in your state that lend devices and help pay for them. No account, no cookies, no ads, no affiliate links.
18. image: WhatCanHelp home page with the wordmark, a Find what actually helps tagline, a search box, a Describe your situation link, browse-by-need buttons, and cards for borrowing and funding
19. heading level 2: Describe the situation
20. image: Match, step one of three: a Describe the situation text box with one clinical and one plain-language example, and a privacy note below it
21. The home page leads with a search box. Under it, “Not sure? Describe your situation instead” opens Match, which is still marked Beta. Match starts with one text box: who it’s for, what they’re trying to do, what’s in the way, any constraints. An AT specialist might write “58yo post-CVA, R-hemiparesis, returning to work; needs one-handed Windows input and voice control.” A parent might write “my 8-year-old has trouble communicating at school.” Both go through the same matching, and both get a shortlist with a note on why each product is on it.
22. I tried a structured checkbox flow first. It looked tidy, but the situations people brought me never fit the boxes. Now people write it the way they’d say it, and Claude pulls out the structure on the backend. What they type goes to Anthropic’s API, which doesn’t train on it, and isn’t stored on the server.
23. heading level 2: The catalog
24. image: WhatCanHelp catalog with filters for challenges, product type, platform and ease of setup, a Recently Added row, and product cards with setup-tier badges
25. The catalog filters by what a product helps with, product type, platform, how hard it is to set up, price, age group, manufacturer and funding source. Search understands plain descriptions as well as product names, so “help hearing the TV” finds TV listening systems. Results show as a table or as cards, and a filtered view can be shared as a link or exported as CSV.
26. heading level 2: How hard is it to set up?
27. Every product gets one of four tiers:
28. columnheader: Tier
29. columnheader: Meaning
30. Ready to use
31. cell: Works on its own with no setup or training
32. Setup with instructions
33. cell: Some configuration needed, but a tutorial gets you there
34. Professional guidance helps
35. cell: You can start alone, but a pro gets much better results
36. Professional setup required
37. cell: Needs professional assessment and configuration
38. Whether someone, or the people around them, can set a device up and keep it working matters as much as whether it fits the need. So the tier appears next to every match, on every product page and in the report. Products in the higher tiers point to RESNA’s AT Professional Finder, ASHA ProFind and the state AT Act program.
39. heading level 2: Product pages
40. image: BIGmack product page with price, a Professional guidance helps badge, shortlist and share buttons, a callout linking to professional finders, and an AI-generated summary
41. Each product page leads with price, manufacturer, setup tier and when the entry was last checked. Where a manufacturer doesn’t publish a price, the page shows an estimated range and says so. The summary is written by Claude from the manufacturer’s published material and stored, not generated on each visit, and it’s labeled as AI-generated. Descriptions aim for about an 8th-grade reading level, and AT terms link to the glossary.
42. heading level 2: Glossary
43. image: WhatCanHelp glossary peek drawer open over the term index, showing the screen reader definition with also-known-as aliases and related-term chips like DAISY, JAWS, and NVDA
44. The glossary has more than 130 plain-language definitions, from AAC and switch access to funding programs and credentials like ATP and CLVT. Clicking a term opens a side drawer instead of a new page, and the link to an open term can be shared. Each term page also lists catalog products that use the term. That lookup matches whole words, so OT doesn’t match “robot,” and it ranks products named for the term above ones that only mention it.
45. heading level 2: Borrowing and funding
46. image: WhatCanHelp borrow page titled Borrow Assistive Technology Before You Buy, with a state selector and per-state lending programs listing website, phone, and a verified date
47. Every US state and territory has a federally funded AT Act program, and most lend devices for a few weeks, free or close to it. Trying a device for a month is a much better basis for a decision than a product listing. The borrow directory lists all 56 programs with a phone number, a website and the date each listing was last verified.
48. image: WhatCanHelp funding directory titled Find Assistive Technology Funding Programs in Your State, with a state selector and links to all 56 states and territories
49. The funding pages cover the same 56. Each one opens with the AT Act program, then Medicaid, Vocational Rehabilitation, AT financing loans and ABLE accounts, with what each program does and questions to ask when you call. They’re starting points, not coverage advice. The same data feeds the “How to fund this” panels on product pages and the funding section of the PDF report.
50. heading level 2: Guides and reports
51. image: WhatCanHelp step-by-step guides index with nine cards, from getting a communication device to sensory regulation
52. Getting AT is usually a sequence: understand the options, borrow before buying, narrow the list, line up funding, and bring it to the right meeting. The guides walk that sequence for one kind of need at a time. There are nine now, from communication devices to sensory regulation.
53. The PDF report collects a summary of the situation, the matched products with explanations, their setup tiers and the funding programs that apply. It’s meant to go into a funding packet or an IEP, or to hand to a family. It’s tagged for screen readers, and it cites the WhatCanHelp and manufacturer pages, never a paid link.
54. heading level 2: Accessibility
55. image: The header settings menu open, with theme, increase contrast, text size, line spacing, your state, reduce motion, and keyboard shortcut controls
56. The settings menu covers theme, an increased-contrast mode, text size, line spacing and reduced motion. The site aims for WCAG 2.2 AA, and automated accessibility checks run on key pages with every change. Screen-reader testing is the main open item: VoiceOver testing of the core flows is under way, with NVDA, JAWS and TalkBack after that. The
57. link: accessibility statement
58. lists what’s still open.
59. heading level 2: How it’s built
60. Node.js and Express, SQLite, and plain JavaScript in the browser. The Anthropic API writes product descriptions, classifies products and runs Match. Product data comes from a scraper framework that reads manufacturer and retailer sites, hashes each page to spot changes, and refreshes the oldest entries first.
61. Products are tagged across seven dimensions: need, product type, platform, setup tier, price band, funding eligibility and age range. A product can sit under several needs without being duplicated.
62. image: WhatCanHelp data pipeline: scrapers feed product data into SQLite, Claude writes descriptions and classifications, then Match uses that data to produce a shortlist and PDF report.
63. heading level 2: Updates
64. heading level 3: 2026-09-17
65. The state funding pages got a rework. Each of the 56 now opens with the state’s AT Act program, because that is the one call most people should make first: the staff lend devices, demonstrate them, and know which of the other programs fit. Medicaid, Vocational Rehabilitation, AT financing loans and ABLE follow, each with a plain-language summary, a phone number, TTY where the program publishes one, and questions to ask when you call. Listings are compiled from official program sites, the AT3 Center directory and the ABLE National Resource Center, and each shows when it was last verified. The index is at
66. link: whatcanhelp.com/funding
67. .
68. heading level 3: 2026-06-15
69. Three additions around the core recommendation: borrowing, funding, and guided journeys. A borrow directory maps all 56 state and territory AT Act lending programs, so people can try a device before buying it. A funding directory maps the Medicaid, Vocational Rehabilitation, AT Act loan, and ABLE routes for the same 56 jurisdictions, and now drives “How to fund this” panels on products and a funding section in every PDF report. Step-by-step journey guides walk one kind of need (communication, vision, hearing, mobility) through the whole sequence, linking the glossary, lending programs, intake, funding, and report. The catalog grew to 5,502 products across 350+ manufacturers.
70. heading level 3: 2026-05-25
71. Pivoted toward a professional audience (AT specialists, SLPs, OTs, educators) without losing the family-and-self-advocate doorway. Intake collapsed from a multi-step checkbox flow into one freeform “Describe the situation” textarea. Catalog grew to 5,060 products from 23 vendors. Detail pages added price, shortlist + share buttons, and a professional-finder callout pointing to RESNA, ASHA ProFind, and State AT Act Programs. Glossary gained a peek drawer with shareable URLs and a reverse “products that use this term” lookup on term pages. Blog posts now ship with hero images and inline product hover previews. Fresh screenshots throughout.
72. heading level 3: 2026-05-09
73. Live at WhatCanHelp.com. 3,600+ products from 20 vendors browsable with faceted filters, guided intake, PDF reports, glossary, blog, and accessibility controls. Fresh screenshots and project page revision to match the shipped product.
74. heading level 3: 2026-04-26
75. Project page is up. Still in active development - current focus is data cleanup, normalizing product records and testing the intake matching pipeline. Exploring when to publish publicly.
