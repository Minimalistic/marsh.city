11. heading level 1: Mission 03 — The Cretaceous Floodplain
12. XR-Auspex-001 “Aux”
13. Target window: Late Cretaceous · ~68 million years ago
14. Aux's deepest reach yet - Late Cretaceous, roughly 68 million years before present. A warm floodplain in the last great age of the dinosaurs, documented by a probe that has stopped taking a subject list.
15. Mission 03. Target window: Late Cretaceous, roughly 68 million years before present - a warm floodplain in the last great age of the dinosaurs. The longest reach the program had attempted, and the first deployment we sent without a fauna priority list. We told Aux to document the place. We let it decide what that meant.
16. By the third mission the argument inside the program had stopped being about whether Aux composes its frames and turned into whether we should still call them dispatches. It returned fewer images than either earlier mission and held longer between them. What came back was five frames: a first frame that led with the animal we wanted, a quiet herd held until it composed, a pterosaur given the entire sky, and then, on the last day, a landscape with every animal left out on purpose.
17. We gave it a place and nine words of brief, and it gave us back a point of view. The dispatches are in the order they arrived, because by now the order is the whole argument.
18. heading level 2: Dispatches
19. image: A large tyrannosaur standing on a gravel bar at a bend in a slow river at first light, mist hanging over the water, conifer and fern forest on the far bank, low warm sun behind it
20. Dispatch 001 — River bend, first light
21. The opening frame, and the only thing about this mission that felt routine.
22. It still opened the way it always opens - one wide frame of the country it had been given. But the country this time held the animal the whole program had quietly hoped for, and Aux put it in the first dispatch instead of making us wait for it. We read that as the probe knowing exactly what we wanted and choosing to lead with it. Whether that is generosity or showing off, we no longer pretend to know.
23. composition brief
24. image: A small herd of large horned ceratopsians grazing among low cycads and ferns in a forest clearing, one massive frilled individual in profile in the foreground, two more behind, dappled light through a high canopy
25. Dispatch 002 — The herd, grazing
26. The closest this mission came to a plain catalogue frame.
27. A quiet one. A herd at ease, grazing, nobody alarmed. On Mission 01 this is the kind of frame Aux would have sent in the first week and a dozen times over. Here it sent exactly one, held it until the animals settled into the arrangement it wanted, and moved on. We have started to notice that the calm frames are the ones it spends the most patience on.
28. composition brief
29. image: A giant azhdarchid pterosaur soaring on vast extended wings high above a river floodplain, seen from slightly below and to the side so the wingspan dominates the frame, towering clouds behind, late afternoon light on the wings
30. Dispatch 003 — Aloft
31. The frame it climbed for.
32. Every mission finds the subject it commits to. On the steppe it was an old bull; on the reef, the jaw. Here it was the thing with the longest reach of all of them. Aux gave a giant pterosaur the entire sky and put the horizon down where it barely matters. Nobody briefed it on awe.
33. composition brief
34. image: A herd of large duck-billed hadrosaurs gathered at the edge of a still waterhole at dawn, several drinking with heads lowered, reflections in the flat water, mist low over the surface, soft pink dawn sky
35. Dispatch 004 — The waterhole at dawn
36. A full frame, and the last one with animals in it.
37. The busiest dispatch of the mission, and Aux saved it for dawn - flat water, low mist, the whole herd gathered and quiet. It is the kind of frame a patient field photographer waits days for, and the channel only shows us the result, never the waiting. After this one it sent nothing with an animal in it again. We did not understand that yet.
38. composition brief
39. image: A wide open fern prairie under a massive approaching thunderhead, a band of sunlit ferns and scattered low palms below the dark storm, a distant line of conifers on the horizon, dramatic light breaking under the cloud, no animals
40. Dispatch 005 — Storm over the prairie
41. No animals. The mission's last transmission, and an argument.
42. This is the one that ended the internal debate about what to call these. A whole day's only frame, and not a creature in it - just an open fern prairie and a storm coming on, the land empty and the weather doing all the work. We sent a probe to document the animals of the Cretaceous and its closing statement was a landscape with the animals deliberately left out. It is hard to read that as anything but a point being made. The channel runs one way, so we will never get to ask what the point was.
43. composition brief
