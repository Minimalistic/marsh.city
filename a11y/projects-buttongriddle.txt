15. ButtonGriddle turns a tablet into a set of choice boards - one per context (meals, activities, clothing) - where each button is a photo and a phrase. Tap a button and the tablet speaks it aloud. A Yes / No / Not sure check screen helps staff confirm understanding in the moment.
16. image: The Meals board - a grid of colored choice buttons; tapping one speaks it aloud
17. It supports communication, but it isn’t an AAC or speech-generating app. The goal is easier participation: fewer spoken directions to process, clearer choices, less guessing on both sides. Whoever supports the person - an OT, SLP, teacher, or family member - builds the boards in a passcode-protected edit mode: photos of the person’s actual items, custom phrases, drag-to-arrange grids. Day to day, staff just present choices.
18. image: The Yes / No / Not sure check screen - three large buttons staff use to confirm understanding
19. Boards are organized by context, so the tablet opens to a simple list - staff pick the situation, the person picks the choice.
20. image: The board list - Meals, Drinks & Snacks, Activities, and Comfort context boards
21. heading level 2: How it’s built
22. Vanilla JavaScript PWA - no framework, no build step, no server, no dependencies. All storage is IndexedDB on the device, including photos; nothing is collected and there’s no account. Works fully offline after install.
